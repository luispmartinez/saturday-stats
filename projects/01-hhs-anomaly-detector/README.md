**Question.** Which months of Texas Medicaid and CHIP enrollment are unusual, and does the choice of method change the answer?

**Finding.** From Oct to Dec 2023, statewide enrollment fell 5% to 6% each month. Median and MAD flagged all three months; mean and SD flagged none, because earlier extreme months stretched its yardstick (for Oct 2023 the standard deviation was about 9 times the MAD). The dashboard shows this for the six risk groups and the state, and lets you click any flagged month to see how the flag works in four steps.

**Method.** Compare each month with the 12 before it: find the center (mean or median), measure the spread (standard deviation or 1.4826 x MAD), score the month (gap divided by spread), flag it past 3. A third view adds a minimum gap in points. Shared logic: [`shared/flagging.py`](../../shared/flagging.py); concept note: [`docs/concepts/01-median-vs-mean.md`](../../docs/concepts/01-median-vs-mean.md).

**What it cannot say.** It shows who moved, not why. Continuous coverage ended Mar 31, 2023 and HHSC data show removals beginning in June 2023, which is context, not cause. HHS marks Feb to Jul 2026 as incomplete. 4,196 caseloads (0.10%) have no county recorded and are not on the map. Independent analysis of public data, not an HHSC product.

**Sources.** HHSC Healthcare Statistics (hhs.texas.gov); HHSC unwinding plan (Jun 2023); Every Texan (Jul 2023); US Census Bureau cartographic boundary files (county).

## Notes for developers
- The dashboard is one Tableau sheet drawn with MAKEPOINT from one long CSV (`layer` column); text alignment is computed in Python, never in Tableau (`PX_PER_PT` is calibrated to Open Sans).
- Counties join by **name**, not FIPS: nine counties (Madison through McMullen) do not follow the HHSC-to-Census code formula.
- Tableau wiring: parameters `p.Group`, `p.Month`, `p.Show Method`; three parameter actions; one calc per layer, generated by `hhs_geom_calcs.py`.
